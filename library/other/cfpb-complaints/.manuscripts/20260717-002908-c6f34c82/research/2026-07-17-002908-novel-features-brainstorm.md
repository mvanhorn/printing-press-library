## Customer model

### Monday compliance analyst

**Today (without this CLI):** A bank or fintech compliance analyst opens the CFPB explorer, a spreadsheet, and one or more saved notebook queries every Monday. They manually reconcile complaint volume, product and issue mix, response types, timeliness, and selected narratives for their company, but cannot reproduce the exact cohort reliably week over week.

**Weekly ritual:** Run a company pulse for the latest reporting window, compare it with the prior window, and collect examples for the compliance review.

**Frustration:** Turning several searches and exports into one reproducible, caveated briefing takes most of the review time.

### Consumer advocate

**Today (without this CLI):** A consumer advocate exports complaints for several companies, filters them into a common product, state, and date cohort, and builds comparison tables manually. They must repeatedly explain that complaint counts are not market-adjusted rates.

**Weekly ritual:** Compare peer response patterns and published narratives within a tightly defined cohort.

**Frustration:** Cohort drift and missing denominator caveats make otherwise careful comparisons easy to misstate.

### Investigative journalist

**Today (without this CLI):** A journalist keeps CFPB searches, CSV downloads, and ad-hoc scripts for named companies and products. They rerun term counts and issue aggregations, then inspect narratives individually to decide whether an apparent change is worth reporting.

**Weekly ritual:** Check for newly growing issues, products, and narrative terms, then preserve complaint IDs supporting a lead.

**Frustration:** The API exposes current evidence but does not provide a reproducible local baseline for deciding what is newly emerging.

### Product-operations lead

**Today (without this CLI):** A product-operations lead searches recent complaints, copies selected narrative examples into internal notes, and avoids material that might encourage identity inference. Narrative availability is opt-in and redacted, so missing narratives are easily mistaken for missing complaints.

**Weekly ritual:** Review representative published narratives and response outcomes for a product area without exposing or inferring individual identities.

**Frustration:** Producing a safe, traceable evidence packet requires repetitive filtering, sampling, caveat writing, and ID capture.

### Killed candidates

| Feature | Kill reason | Closest-surviving-sibling |
|---------|-------------|---------------------------|
| Complaint leaderboard | Ranking companies by raw complaint counts would imply quality without market-share denominators. | `compare companies` |
| Narrative sentiment | Sentiment and semantic summarization require an LLM or unsupported NLP dependency; exact-term change is the verifiable mechanical alternative. | `emerging themes` |
| Market-adjusted complaint rate | The API provides no customer, account, or market-share denominator, so the requested rate cannot be computed honestly. | `compare companies` |
| Continuous alert daemon | Persistent polling and notification delivery require background infrastructure beyond a one-command CLI feature. | `watch changes` |
| Causal spike explainer | Complaint records support descriptive changes but cannot verify causal explanations. | `emerging themes` |
| Geographic hotspot map | Raw state counts invite denominator overclaims, while a simple geographic aggregation is already table stakes. | `compare companies` |
| Response benchmark | Its response and timeliness fields are more useful inside the shared-cohort peer comparison than as an overlapping command. | `compare companies` |
| Consumer identity resolver | Identity inference is unsupported by the API and conflicts with the redaction and privacy requirements. | `narratives packet` |
