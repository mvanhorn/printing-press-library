## Customer model

**Mike — Toolbox Architect (net-worth flow).** Today he runs two hand-built MCP servers side by side: a read-only `ynab-mcp` (list_budgets, get_account_balances, list_categories, get_transactions, etc.) and a `projectionlab-mcp` whose `sync_ynab_balances` tool pushes YNAB balances into ProjectionLab through an explicit, hand-maintained account-name mapping. His weekly ritual is pulling current YNAB account balances and pushing them into ProjectionLab to keep the household net-worth projection current, then manually cross-checking WPCU/Betterment/Fidelity balances by hand since those aren't API-connected yet. His frustration: two brittle, hand-built servers to maintain, no historical trend data because every read hits the live API fresh, and an account-name mapping that's invisible glue code he has to remember to update whenever an account changes.

**Mike — Pre-Purchase Category Checker.** Today, mid-errand or mid-checkout, he asks his existing read-only `ynab-mcp` (or opens the YNAB app) "what's left in Dining Out this month" before deciding whether to buy something. His weekly ritual is several of these quick category-balance checks per week, glancing at overall available funds before discretionary purchases. His frustration: the read-only tool gives a point-in-time snapshot with no sense of pace — it can't tell him "at this rate you'll overspend Dining Out by the 20th," and there's no local history to compare this month's trajectory against last month's at the same point.

**Claude (agent) — Transaction Entry & Approval Delegate.** Today Mike delegates transaction logging and approval of bank-synced pending transactions to an agent through thin MCP tools (per the Codebase Intelligence: `calebl/ynab-mcp-server`-style surfaces expose only ListBudgets/BudgetSummary/GetUnapprovedTransactions/CreateTransaction/ApproveTransaction). Its weekly ritual is batch-reviewing unapproved transactions synced in from linked bank accounts, approving the clearly-correct ones, and occasionally splitting a transaction (e.g., a Costco run split between groceries and household). Its frustration: no category-funding or reconciliation tools exist in the current agent-facing surface, so it has to punt those two Top Workflows back to the human/UI, breaking the "let the agent handle it" promise the household actually wants.

**Mike — Monthly Reconciler & Trend Reviewer.** Today he reconciles a YNAB account balance against a bank statement roughly monthly, comparing line by line until he finds the discrepancy transaction, and reviews spending trends by category/payee by scrolling YNAB's own in-app reports since no CLI/agent tool exposes month-over-month data. His ritual: pull the statement, hunt for the mismatch; near month-end, eyeball category spend vs. budget to catch drift before it becomes a habit. His frustration: reconciliation is manual and error-prone, and trend review means either trusting YNAB's own uninspectable charts or burning API calls against the 200/hour rate limit to recompute totals by hand across months.
