1. Are the 20 generated operations callable directly, or only through policy-enforcing commands? If `messages.batchModify`, `threads.trash`, or `labels.delete` is exposed as a raw subcommand, the binary-path allowlist permits the assistant to bypass planning, confirmation, and the ledger with different arguments. Every mutation needs a central capability check, or the raw operations must be unreachable from the CLI.

2. The “Trash-only ceiling” does not cover labels. `labels.delete` immediately and permanently deletes a label and removes it from every message and thread; recreating its name produces a different immutable ID and does not inherently restore memberships. This contradicts the blanket claim that label operations have inverses. Why include deletion when the adopted requirement only names creating folders? Prune it or specify its separate irreversible-operation protocol. [Google labels.delete documentation](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.labels/delete)

3. The RFC 8058 classifier is incomplete and turns attacker-controlled email headers into network requests. RFC 8058 requires a valid DKIM signature covering both unsubscribe headers, forbids cookies/authentication context, and says senders must not redirect; the design mentions none of those checks. It also lacks private/loopback/link-local address blocking, DNS-rebinding defense, response-size limits, and deadlines. A crafted email could therefore make the Mac POST to an unrelated or internal HTTPS service. [RFC 8058 requirements](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058.html)

4. What is the crash-consistency protocol across Gmail mutation, ledger commit, and reporting? `batchModify` can cover 1,000 IDs and returns an empty success body; across chunks, a kill or timeout after Gmail committed but before the ledger commit leaves an unknown or unrecorded mutation. The same ambiguity applies to an unsubscribe POST whose response is lost. Require a durable write-ahead state machine, reconciliation before retry, idempotent chunk identities, and fault-injection tests at every boundary. [Gmail batchModify documentation](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages/batchModify)

5. What exactly does a confirmation authorize? The plan must freeze and bind the profile/account, exact message IDs, exact label IDs and deltas, preview digest, expiry, and one-time nonce—not merely a query or mutable plan filename. The code must be consumed transactionally and matched as the entire authenticated owner message. Otherwise a stale/replayed code, edited plan file, account mix-up, or newly matching message can authorize work the owner never previewed.

6. Undo semantics ignore concurrent mailbox changes. If the owner or Gmail changes labels between planning, applying, and undoing, blindly replaying inverse operations can overwrite those later choices. Capture fresh per-message preimages immediately before mutation, record only deltas actually introduced, and make undo detect/report post-state conflicts instead of forcing the inverse. This matters especially because history records expose individual label additions/removals and cursors may expire within hours, so the local snapshot cannot be assumed current. [Gmail history documentation](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.history/list)

7. The two explicitly cited operational failures remain absent from the build design. Where are the persisted “ran today” catch-up check, single-instance lock, producer exit-status preservation through pipelines, and failure notification? Without them, a reboot can again suppress the daily preview, or a failed CLI piped into a successful formatter/messenger can report apparent success.

VERDICT: REVISE
