## Customer model

**Riley — roommate-household bill-runner.** Riley runs a standing Splitwise group for a shared apartment: rent, utilities, groceries, the occasional shared Costco run. Three to four housemates, one payer per bill, equal or near-equal splits.

*Today (without this CLI):* Riley opens the Splitwise app, taps into the household group, scrolls the expense feed to see whether the gas bill got logged this month, then taps each housemate's name to see what they owe. There is no way to see *how long* a balance has been sitting; the app shows a number, not an age. Riley keeps a mental list of "who still hasn't paid me for June."

*Weekly ritual:* Sunday evening — check that every recurring bill for the cycle is in the group, check the net position per housemate, and decide whom to nudge. Once a month, look at what the household spent on groceries vs utilities.

*Frustration:* Splitwise has no recurrence concept and no aging. A missed bill is invisible until someone asks; a housemate who "always settles eventually" looks identical to one who settled yesterday. Riley wants the staleness of a debt, not just its size, and wants the reminder to go out as a comment on the actual expense thread, not a separate text.

**Sam — trip organizer / treasurer.** Sam fronts the Airbnb, the rental car, and the group dinners for a 6–10 person trip, logs everything into a trip group, and reconciles at the end.

*Today (without this CLI):* Sam relies on Splitwise's "simplify debts" toggle and hopes the numbers are right. To audit, Sam scrolls the trip's entire expense list and mentally re-adds it, because the app only ever shows the current snapshot, never the running history. Duplicate "Settle all balances" rows from two people tapping settle at once have silently corrupted a trip before.

*Weekly ritual:* During the trip, log expenses daily and sanity-check the per-head running total. At trip end: audit for duplicates and cost outliers, produce a per-person / per-category summary the group can read, and compute the fewest Venmo transfers to zero everyone out — then record those settlements.

*Frustration:* The API returns balances per group and per friend but no ledger and no transfer plan; the app's settle-up is per-pair. Sam needs an auditable running ledger and a minimal-transfer plan that can be previewed before it is recorded — and needs to be certain the local copy is complete (a prior sync bug silently dropped 43 of 143 expenses).

**Devon — couple / personal-finance tracker.** Devon shares expenses with a partner across a home group, two travel groups, and a pile of non-group one-offs, in three currencies (home, plus two trips abroad), and has old IOUs with friends scattered outside any group.

*Today (without this CLI):* Devon exports nothing (Splitwise's CSV export is per-group), searches the app's weak search box for "that dinner in Lisbon," and hand-converts EUR and JPY totals in a spreadsheet to see real monthly joint spend. Balances with the same person span several groups, so the true net owed is never shown in one place.

*Weekly ritual:* Find a specific past expense by keyword; look at monthly spend trend by category across *all* groups; occasionally collapse cross-group balances with the partner or a friend to one real-world transfer.

*Frustration:* No cross-group netting, no offline full-history search, no multi-currency normalization. Devon wants one net number per person and one base-currency spend series, with unconverted currencies surfaced honestly rather than dropped.

**Avery — agent operator.** Avery drives the CLI from an LLM (Claude via the MCP server or shell tools) to answer "what do I owe," "settle the trip," "how much did we spend on food" on the user's behalf.

*Today (without this CLI):* An endpoint-mirror MCP fans out `get_groups` + `get_friends` + paged `get_expenses` per question, and each response is a deeply nested members/shares array (the `get-groups` list tool was a ~119KB firehose the host rejected). Large IDs rendered in scientific notation produced spurious 403s. Choosing between `balances`, `debts`, `net`, `fairness`, and `settle-up` for one natural-language request is a coin flip without scope redirects.

*Weekly ritual:* Every session starts with "what is the user's current money state" and ends with a write action (record a split, record a settlement, post a nudge) that must be previewed before it is committed.

*Frustration:* Token bloat from live fan-out and nested arrays; tool descriptions that don't say which sibling to use; `--compact` that drops the one field (`balance`) an agent actually needs; a `sql` tool whose schema description lies about the store shape.
