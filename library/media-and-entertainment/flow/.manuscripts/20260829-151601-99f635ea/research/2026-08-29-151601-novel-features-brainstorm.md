## Customer model

**Mara — the audio-drama animator.** *Today:* Mara has her mp3 audio-drama script open in a text editor next to a rough, hand-timed beat sheet, a folder of seed images (really synced from a shared Drive folder she has to download-then-reupload one file at a time, since Flow has no Drive picker), and a Flow tab. She hand-types a prompt for every single beat — 20 to 40 per episode — manually staging the matching image each time. She has no way to check a whole planned batch against her remaining credits before she starts clicking through it. Critically, Flow has no mechanism anywhere to import or sync her real mp3 to a generation. *Weekly ritual:* every time a new episode script/mp3 is ready, she spends an evening turning timed beats into individual Flow prompts by hand, submitting them one at a time, then — after everything renders — opens a separate video editor to lay her real audio back over the silent/Veo-audio clips in the right order. *Frustration:* hand-prompting every shot is the most tedious part of her week, and because Flow will never accept her real audio, she's guaranteed a manual re-assembly pass no matter what — she wants that final muxing step to stop being manual too.

**Devon — the credit-conscious batch producer.** *Today:* Devon runs several client projects against a shared Pro/Ultra credit pool. He keeps a rough mental tally of Veo tier costs from experience and finds out in the moment whether he actually has enough credits by clicking a generation and watching whether it fails. He has no offline way to review a whole day's planned batch and know its total cost before clicking through it one at a time. *Weekly ritual:* before starting a client's batch for the week, he wants to know at a glance what's queued and whether the balance covers it, instead of discovering three-quarters of the way through Monday's client work that Wednesday's project is going to come up short. *Frustration:* nowhere — not Flow's UI, not any community tool — will tell him "this batch costs X, you have Y, here's what to cut" before he starts spending.

**Priya — the Drive-based asset librarian.** *Today:* Priya's character sheets and scene mood-boards live in a Drive folder tree organized by episode and scene number (e.g. `Scene03_close_up.png`). To get any of them into Flow she downloads each file out of Drive individually, then re-uploads it through Flow's native OS file dialog — there is no in-app Drive picker anywhere in the product. Once an image is in a Flow project, nothing tracks which scene or character it was meant for; she re-derives that from memory or the filename every time she stages a generation. *Weekly ritual:* at the start of each episode's animation pass, she pulls the week's new reference images out of Drive, stages them into the right Flow project, and manually cross-references her own notes to remember which image belongs to which shot. *Frustration:* the download-then-reupload dance for every asset is pure friction, and even once an image is in Flow she's on her own to remember what it was for.

**Sam — the queue-and-walk-away producer.** *Today:* Sam queues several generations before stepping away and expects a straightforward answer to "is everything done, and did anything fail?" when he comes back. Right now that means reopening the Flow tab and clicking into each asset individually, or babysitting one generation's status at a time. *Weekly ritual:* kick off a batch, walk away, come back later, want one glance at aggregate progress across the whole batch rather than N separate look-ups. *Frustration:* checking on a batch today means N manual status checks instead of one, with no local record of "this is the batch I submitted" to check progress against.
