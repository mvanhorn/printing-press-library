## Customer model

### The weekly outing traveler

**Today (without this CLI):** A wheelchair traveler opens WheeLog's public map and the canonical pages for toilets, elevators, ramps, and entrances relevant to an upcoming outing. They read category-specific questions and their positive and negative counts, then check the spot's recorded and updated dates. These habits are inferred from the brief's first Users entry and Top Workflows 1–2; no retained contributor profile or interview is claimed.

**Weekly ritual:** Before the next week's outings, they check whether a few candidate places have affirmative, contrary, conflicting, or missing reports for the equipment questions they care about. They keep a small set of useful public places available between planning sessions.

**Frustration:** A place's broad category cannot answer their equipment question, and a missing or conflicting answer is easy to lose while switching between spot pages.

### The Japan itinerary planner

**Today (without this CLI):** A planner works through WheeLog search results and several attraction, hotel, and station pages while assembling a Japan itinerary. They manually line up the questions relevant to their party, preserve the original place names and canonical links, and note gaps. The brief's second Users entry and Top Workflows 1–3 ground this persona; weekly planning describes the recurring ritual being served, rather than an asserted interview finding.

**Weekly ritual:** In their recurring itinerary-planning session, they discover a bounded set of candidate places and compare a few exact spot IDs against explicit equipment questions. They repeat the same evidence check as the shortlist changes.

**Frustration:** Search results do not provide a trustworthy comparison of the requested question evidence, and an attractive-looking result can have no relevant reports.

### The shortlist assistant

**Today (without this CLI):** An assistant revisits the canonical WheeLog pages for the same public-place shortlist between planning sessions. They copy allowed facility facts, aggregate question counts, and dates into their own notes and try to remember what changed. This follows the brief's third Users entry, Top Workflow 4, and the Data Layer's bounded normalized cache.

**Weekly ritual:** In each week's shortlist-maintenance session, they check changes in saved spot evidence and identify entries that need another look before the next outing. They maintain only selected public facility facts and links.

**Frustration:** They cannot easily distinguish a changed aggregate answer from an unchanged answer that was merely fetched again, or prioritize records whose evidence is missing or whose update date is old or unknown.

#### 1. Requested-question evidence matrix

1. **Weekly use:** Yes. The weekly outing traveler repeats this comparison before that week's outing, and the Japan itinerary planner repeats it during their weekly shortlist session.
2. **Wrapper vs leverage:** It expands multiple exact IDs and aligns requested question IDs and states across records; no single source endpoint returns this comparison. It does not repeat the absorbed single-spot inspection.
3. **Transcendence proof:** Service-specific category/question identities and agent-shaped output produce an evidence matrix that explicitly distinguishes affirmative-only, negative-only, conflicting, zero-vote, absent, and category-inapplicable observations. Question identity, rather than matching label text, controls alignment. Categories without the requested question remain inapplicable or unknown unless the verified source question mapping establishes applicability; no invented cross-category equivalence is introduced.
4. **Sibling kill:** Chair-clearance comparator was killed because the source has no typed measurements; this matrix preserves exactly the aggregate question evidence that exists.
5. **Buildability:** `hand-code`. A bounded multi-ID orchestration and custom matrix output require a Cobra behavior plus `root.go` wiring beyond endpoint mirroring, approximately 100–150 lines using shared normalized-detail helpers.
6. **Long-description validity:** `spots search` and `shortlist list` both survive with those exact command names; the --audit redirect distinguishes saved-list maintenance from explicit-ID comparison.

Implementation source declares exactly one `// pp:data-source auto` directive and honors `--data-source auto|local|live`: attempt live reads with labeled local fallback in auto, read saved observations only in local, and require real API reads in live. Enforce a small explicit ID bound. Requested affirmative requirements yield `reported-affirmative`, `reported-negative`, `conflicting`, `unreported`, or `inapplicable`, with underlying source counts retained; they never yield an unqualified accessibility pass. If a local-store plan needs child questions, drain the spot rows into structs, check `rows.Err()`, close rows, then query the questions. Use `hintIfUnsynced` followed by `hintIfStale` when returning local results. An absent cached ID is a missing observation, not an empty facility.

#### 2. Question-evidence discovery

1. **Weekly use:** Yes. The Japan itinerary planner uses it in each weekly planning session to reduce candidate pages to a bounded evidence triage, and the outing traveler uses it when choosing that week's facilities.
2. **Wrapper vs leverage:** It performs bounded search plus actual detail expansion and verifies requested evidence states; it does more than forwarding the source's question-array parameter.
3. **Transcendence proof:** WheeLog's category-specific aggregate question content and agent-shaped bucket output make affirmative, contrary, conflicting, and unreported candidates visible together with checked coverage. `questionList[]` acceptance alone is not used as proof of an answer or of AND/OR filter semantics.
4. **Sibling kill:** Positive-only question search wrapper was killed because it both renamed an endpoint and falsely promoted request filtering to confirmed affirmative evidence.
5. **Buildability:** `hand-code`. A shared-detail-backed search option needs bounded expansion, per-candidate question evaluation, and coverage metadata, approximately 100–150 lines rather than a second search command tree.
6. **Long-description validity:** `spots compare` and `shortlist list` survive with those exact names; the --origin redirect distinguishes saved-list proximity lookup from live keyword discovery.

Extend the existing `spots search` behavior instead of registering another search command. Its source declares exactly one `// pp:data-source auto` directive and honors live versus local data. In live/auto mode, enforce explicit page and detail-count bounds, echo the actual keyword/category/UTC-date request, and show listed, checked, unavailable, and unexpanded counts without implying exhaustive regional coverage. In local mode, apply the same question classifier to saved matching spots and state the saved-list scope; use the generated sync/staleness hints and the drain-first SQLite read pattern. A failed detail fetch produces an unavailable state; it is neither an affirmative result nor a zero-vote observation. Explicit source question IDs avoid NLP and preserve natural worldwide keyword inputs.

#### 3. Saved-evidence changes

1. **Weekly use:** Yes. The shortlist assistant checks the same selected places between weekly planning sessions and needs to identify exactly which recorded facts changed.
2. **Wrapper vs leverage:** A source detail response contains the current record; it cannot identify differences from the assistant's earlier saved observation. The local baseline supplies the missing comparison.
3. **Transcendence proof:** SQLite stores only the previous and latest normalized, allowlisted observations per selected spot. Joining those snapshots by stable spot and question IDs yields changes such as `unreported → reported-affirmative`, a new conflicting vote, or an updated record timestamp, each with old/new counts and observation times.
4. **Sibling kill:** Always-on shortlist watcher was killed because it requires persistent monitoring and a presumed change feed; a bounded foreground difference check supplies the assistant's useful weekly action.
5. **Buildability:** `hand-code`. A small two-observation cache addition, stable-ID diff, and shared detail fetches need custom Cobra logic and wiring, approximately 150–180 lines using shared cache helpers; the minor schema addition is why feasibility scores 1.
6. **Long-description validity:** `shortlist list` survives with that exact name; its --audit behavior performs local maintenance triage without a refresh.

Source declares exactly one `// pp:data-source auto` directive. Auto attempts real detail fetches and can fall back to the last stored observation pair with an explicit fallback/source status; live requires source fetch success; local compares the stored pair and labels it as the last recorded transition with its two observation times. A successful live comparison first compares the cached latest observation with the fetched normalized result, then rotates the bounded pair. Unavailable responses never replace a valid baseline or imply deletion. New-to-cache records receive `no-baseline`; unchanged records receive `unchanged`. Changes in vote counts describe reported evidence, never a physical improvement or deterioration. Local paths use sync/staleness hints and drain-first reads. For writes, use helpers participating in the same `*sql.Tx`, or commit custom-table writes before pooled `store.Upsert`/`store.UpsertBatch`; never nest their transactions or ignore upsert errors. No contributor identity or narrative enters either snapshot.

#### 4. Shortlist recheck queue

1. **Weekly use:** Yes. The shortlist assistant runs it at the start of each weekly maintenance session to decide which source pages need attention, and the traveler checks the saved plan before that week's outing.
2. **Wrapper vs leverage:** It combines saved membership, requested question evidence, and two distinct clocks across the selected list; no endpoint returns this recheck queue.
3. **Transcendence proof:** Local SQLite joins and agent-shaped reason codes expose why each selected record needs rechecking. The --audit behavior sorts deterministically by missing cached detail, missing/conflicting requested evidence, old or unknown source-update dates, and cache observation age while returning every reason rather than a single suitability score.
4. **Sibling kill:** Universal wheelchair-suitability score was killed because it collapses evidence into an unsupported personal-safety verdict; a reasoned recheck queue makes the uncertainty actionable without inventing that verdict.
5. **Buildability:** `hand-code`. A local join, UTC age calculation, reason vector, and deterministic ordering need hand-written --audit behavior in the existing shortlist list command, approximately 100–150 lines using existing saved spot/question tables and shared readers.
6. **Long-description validity:** `shortlist changes`, `spots search`, and `spots compare` survive with those exact names. Both shortlist-list behaviors share one Cobra Long description that also identifies --audit and --origin, so a single command receives consistent scope guidance.

Source declares exactly one `// pp:data-source local` directive and rejects `--data-source live` with a clear `no live equivalent` error. Call generated sync/staleness hints before local results. Drain and close any saved-membership rows before fetching normalized question rows. Source `updated` age is unknown when the source leaves it empty; show `created` separately and do not silently treat it as a recent update. `observed_at` measures cache age only. Aggregate question counts lack individual observation dates, so the command must not label a question itself fresh merely because the record's update date is recent. Missing question data, zero/zero counts, mixed votes, and unavailable cached detail remain different queue reasons. Required-question choices use exact IDs and do not infer equivalent wording across categories.

#### 5. Offline nearest saved equipment

1. **Weekly use:** Yes. The weekly outing traveler checks the nearest saved toilet, elevator, or ramp around the next outing's explicit location, including when the bounded shortlist must work offline.
2. **Wrapper vs leverage:** The source endpoints do not return a nearest-neighbor answer over the traveler's selected offline places. Local coordinates, selected membership, and question evidence produce that answer.
3. **Transcendence proof:** A local saved-membership/question join plus a deterministic Haversine calculation produces ordered straight-line distances and per-question states without geocoding or any outside location service. A supplied origin and saved facilities are enough; coverage is explicitly limited to the saved shortlist.
4. **Sibling kill:** Automatically merged venue-facility bundle was killed because nearby coordinates do not prove venue membership or usable connections; this survivor reports only spatial distance between public facility coordinates.
5. **Buildability:** `hand-code`. A bounded local read, validated coordinate/radius inputs, Haversine calculation, and evidence-bearing output need hand-written --origin behavior in the existing shortlist list command, approximately 100–150 lines with the standard library and shared local readers.
6. **Long-description validity:** `shortlist changes`, `spots search`, and `spots compare` survive with those exact names. The same shared shortlist-list Long description identifies --origin versus --audit and redirects unsaved keyword discovery to spots search.

Source declares exactly one `// pp:data-source local` directive and rejects `--data-source live` with a clear `no live equivalent` error. Call generated sync/staleness hints, drain and close parent rows before question queries, and keep JSON/stdout clean. Validate latitude/longitude ranges, finite numbers, and a bounded nonnegative radius. Records without valid coordinates remain explicitly unlocatable and are counted rather than assigned zero distance. Distances are labeled `straight_line_distance_m`; output says `coverage: saved_shortlist` and makes no path, entrance linkage, venue ownership, availability, or wheelchair-route claim. Exact category values and question IDs retain original source meaning. The supplied origin is used for this invocation only and is not persisted.
