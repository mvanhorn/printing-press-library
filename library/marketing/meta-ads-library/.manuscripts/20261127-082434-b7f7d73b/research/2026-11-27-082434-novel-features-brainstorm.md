## Customer model

### 1. Elena — DTC Creative Strategist at a 12-Person E-Commerce Brand

- **Today (without this CLI):** Every Monday morning before the creative sprint standup, Elena opens 8–10 pinned tabs of `facebook.com/ads/library/` filtered to her top US and Mexico DTC competitors. She scrolls endlessly through dynamic creative cards, copies promising opening lines into a Notion swipe file, hovers over obfuscated `l.facebook.com` links to see where ads land, and manually eyeballs start dates to guess which creatives have survived past 30 days. She cannot easily answer which ads launched or died since last Monday, or which opening hooks are backed by the highest active collation counts across all 8 competitors.
- **Weekly ritual:** Every Monday, Elena audits her competitor set to spot proven evergreen winners (30/60/90+ days running), note newly launched creative tests, extract recurring opening copy hooks and CTAs, and pull 5–10 reference creatives to brief her video editors and copywriters.
- **Frustration:** Meta's Ad Library web UI shows only a flat, ephemeral snapshot with no sort-by-longevity, no historical changelog of what launched or got paused since last week, and no deduplication of repeated ad copy across collated variants—forcing 2 hours of repetitive scrolling every week.

### 2. Marcus — Paid Social Media Buyer at a Performance Agency

- **Today (without this CLI):** Marcus manages paid social across Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and Messenger for five client accounts. To build Friday client reports and new-business pitch decks, he exports partial CSVs using brittle Python scrapers or manually tallies active ad counts, video-vs-image ratios, and landing page URLs in Google Sheets. Because `l.facebook.com` wraps every destination URL and Meta provides no cross-advertiser view, he cannot quickly benchmark 3–5 rival brands side by side or see which competitor owns share of voice on a client's core category keyword.
- **Weekly ritual:** Every Friday (and ahead of pitch meetings), Marcus benchmarks each client against 2–4 direct competitors on active creative volume, format/platform mix, refresh velocity, CTA distribution, and landing-page/UTM funnel destinations, while scanning category keywords to catch emerging entrants.
- **Frustration:** Comparing multiple advertisers or mapping where competitors send paid traffic (quizzes, advertorials, bundle offers, UTM structures) requires tedious multi-tab scraping and manual spreadsheet pivots that go stale the next day.

### 3. Priya — Digital Transparency & Political Ad Researcher

- **Today (without this CLI):** Priya monitors EU/UK regulated advertisers and political/issue spenders for a weekly policy brief. She juggles two disconnected workflows: Python scripts against Meta's official `v27.0/ads_archive` Graph API (which frequently break on EU-only field permissions and return empty arrays for non-EU commercial ads) and manual browser lookups on `facebook.com/ads/library/` to click into individual ad transparency panels for beneficiary/payer disclosures and age-by-gender reach breakdowns. She cannot aggregate payer entities, demographic skew, or violation/AI-disclosure flags across an entire advertiser without stitching JSON exports by hand.
- **Weekly ritual:** Every week, Priya pulls updated ads for tracked political pages, bylines, and large EU/UK commercial advertisers to verify beneficiary/payer consistency, quantify demographic and regional reach concentration, sum spend/impression brackets, and flag removed or policy-violating creatives.
- **Frustration:** Transparency data is fragmented across single-ad GraphQL detail panels (`AdLibraryV3AdDetailsQuery`) and permission-sensitive official `ads_archive` records, with no single command that reconciles and rolls up an advertiser's disclosures locally.

### Killed candidates

| Feature | Kill Reason | Closest Surviving Sibling |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-classified ad angle tagging (`angles`) | Killed inline for LLM dependency: classifying UGC vs founder story vs problem-solution requires semantic/vision inference and produces noisy results mechanically; users should pipe `hooks --json` to an LLM. | `hooks` |
| Live landing page health & offer scraper (`landing-check`) | Killed inline for external service dependency and scope creep: crawling third-party advertiser websites is outside Meta Ad Library, hits WAFs/bot walls, and belongs in a separate crawler. | `funnels` |
| Collation family tree & variant inspector (`variants`) | Cut for low weekly standalone frequency: strategists inspect winning creatives and top hooks across an account rather than drilling into single `collation_id` micro-diffs every week. | `winners` |
| Creative format & placement matrix (`placements`) | Cut as a narrow single-slice report whose core value (video/image/carousel and FB/IG/Threads/Messenger mix) is already delivered in context by `compare` and `sov`. | `compare` |
| Watchlist batch snapshotter (`watchlist`) | Cut for scope creep and framework overlap: maintaining local watchlist CRUD and a multi-page polling scheduler builds a mini-app that duplicates `diff --refresh` and framework `sync`. | `diff` |
| Political byline spend rollup (`bylines`) | Cut for optional-auth dependence and sibling overlap: political byline spend bounds rely heavily on `META_ACCESS_TOKEN` `ads_archive` rows and are cleanly subsumed by `transparency --byline`. | `transparency` |
| Creative refresh cadence & cohort survival timeline (`velocity`) | Cut because standalone weekly launch histograms see lower weekly use than concrete winning creatives (`winners`), snapshot deltas (`diff`), and 7d/30d velocity inside `compare`. | `compare` |
